#### A Unified Model for the Gravitational Wave Background and Quasar Luminosity Functions
 - **Authors:** Cayenne Matt, Kayhan Gültekin, Ryan Hickox
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28680

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28680

 - **Abstract**
 The supermassive black hole mass function (BHMF) is a necessary ingredient in our understanding of how BHs grow and evolve with their host galaxies. Estimations of the BHMF based on observations of local BHs, quasars, and gravitational wave data yield inconsistent results, leading to large uncertainties in, e.g., BH accretion and seeding models. The nanohertz gravitational wave background (GWB) has been a powerful tool to provide constraints on the BHMF across cosmic time. We take posterior BHMFs that describe the GWB and fit them to bolometric quasar luminosity functions (QLF) across $0.2 \leq z \leq 6.2$ to constrain the Eddington ratio distribution function (ERDF). Compared to a fiducial BHMF, based on galaxy and a local black hole mass scaling relation, the BHMFs that match the GWB can reproduce the observed QLFs with lower Eddington fractions and without requiring significant super-Eddington accretion. We use a double power-law form of the ERDF and find that the fits using the GWB-inferred BHMF have systematically lower break-points and marginally steeper slopes in the high-Eddington fraction portion of the function. Furthermore, the best-fit QLFs using the GWB-inferred BHMFs provide better fits to the high-luminosity end of the QLF data than the fiducial model. For both models the ERDF shows significant redshift evolution of the break-point which reaches a maximum around $z \sim 2$. Overall, our prescription can produce realistic QLFs from BHMFs and our GWB-based model is consistent with electromagnetic and gravitational wave data sets.
#### The Balmer Absorber Strength Problem of Little Red Dots
 - **Authors:** Hanpu Liu, Eliot Quataert, Bingjie Wang, Jenny E. Greene, Yan-Fei Jiang
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28720

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28720

 - **Abstract**
 Little Red Dots (LRDs) commonly show Balmer absorption features. Some observed LRD spectra have weaker absorption in H$\alpha$ than in H$\beta$, in apparent contradiction to atomic physics according to standard attenuation models. This tension is naturally resolved when one considers emission from the absorbing medium itself, which is ignored in the standard model: when the Balmer absorption is optically thick, a non-negligible source function could fill in the H$\alpha$ absorption trough and reduce its observed absorption equivalent width (EW). With proof-of-concept photoionization models we show that the source function of H$\alpha$ could be systematically stronger than that of H$\beta$. This is due to radiative trapping preferentially populating the $n=3$ level of neutral hydrogen, which operates efficiently when the medium is optically thick to H$\alpha$ but not very optically thick to Paschen-$\alpha$. Such line-dependent source functions could lead to significant corrections to the expected EW and selectively weaken H$\alpha$ absorption, consistent with observations. Quantitatively, this depends on the distance of the absorbing medium and its internal structure, providing a new diagnostic of the environment around LRDs. Future modeling of the absorbers may also potentially open a new avenue for estimating the mass of the central engine.
#### Poisson Clustering and Seed Effect of PBHs: Implications for Microlensing, Gravitational Waves, Little Red Dots and Ultra-Faint Dwarf Galaxies
 - **Authors:** Bernard Carr, Sebastien Clesse, Juan Garcia-Bellido, Michael Hawkins, Florian Kuhnel
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc); High Energy Physics - Phenomenology (hep-ph)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28723

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28723

 - **Abstract**
 The existence of primordial black holes (PBHs) could have significant effects on the formation of cosmic structures through both the seed effect and the Poisson effect. For an extended PBH mass function, this should lead to dark virialised bound clusters of black holes with modest mass, together with central black holes of larger mass. We consider the combination of three effects: the inevitable gravitational binding of small-scale inhomogeneities due to Poisson and seed fluctuations; the dilution through dynamical heating of all clusters smaller than around $10^{6}\,M_{\odot}$; the survival of clusters in the outer Galactic halo (i.e. towards the Magellanic Clouds) but their destruction in the central halo region through tidal disruption and collisions. We examine the implications of this for the constraints on the fraction of dark matter in PBHs from microlensing surveys and gravitational-wave events. Although the clustering of PBHs with the expected mass and radius is unlikely to relax the microlensing limits, the decline of the Galactic rotation curve might do so, allowing much of the dark matter to be in solar-mass PBHs formed at the quantum chromodynamics transition. We also emphasise that binary merger rates must be revised: binaries formed in PBH clusters can dominate the merger rate, which may be compatible with the LIGO-Virgo-KAGRA gravitational-wave limits if PBHs provide the dark matter. More positively, Ultra-Faint Dwarf Galaxies and Little Red Dots might arise through a combination of the seed and Poisson effects. Our conclusions could be modified for PBHs produced from primordial fluctuations with exponential non-Gaussian tails, since the clustering would then exceed the Poissonian prediction, opening up new possibilities to explain dark matter with solar-mass PBHs.
#### Arepo-CD: Coupling Collisional $N$-Body Dynamics with Moving-Mesh Magnetohydrodynamics
 - **Authors:** Chuizheng Kong, Hui Li, Long Wang, Yunwei Deng, Elizabeth Moreno-Hilario
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Instrumentation and Methods for Astrophysics (astro-ph.IM)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28727

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28727

 - **Abstract**
 Star formation is a hierarchical, clustered process in turbulent giant molecular clouds (GMCs), where close stellar encounters can strongly affect cluster evolution and feedback. However, gravitational softening in many hydrodynamic simulations of GMCs limits the treatment of these encounters. We present Arepo-CD (Collisional Dynamics), a framework that couples star-by-star collisional $N$-body dynamics to the moving-mesh magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) code Arepo. Star-star interactions use unsoftened gravity, while interactions involving gas or collisionless components remain softened. Compact stellar subsystems are identified on the fly and evolved with a fourth-order forward symplectic integrator and slow-down algorithmic regularization coupled to the native Arepo timestep hierarchy. Benchmark tests against the collisional $N$-body code PeTar show good agreement in cluster core collapse and subsequent expansion, radial density profiles, and stellar velocity distributions. Coupled to the Rigel star formation and feedback model, Arepo-CD follows cluster formation and early evolution in radiation MHD simulations of a $10^5~M_\odot$ turbulent molecular cloud. In the fiducial GMC comparison, the collisional and softened-gravity runs yield similar star formation histories and integrated star formation efficiencies. Nevertheless, close gravitational encounters during the embedded phase generate a substantial runaway population, with a velocity tail extending beyond 100 km/s. Ejected massive stars redistribute radiative feedback into the surrounding lower-density medium and produce a more extended distribution of heated gas. Arepo-CD provides a practical and computationally efficient framework for studying collisional stellar dynamics and its coupling to gas evolution and feedback during star cluster formation in realistic galactic environments.

 - **Abstract**
 An increasing number of slow-rotating pulsars and long-period transients have been observed in recent years. The periods of these sources can be significantly longer than typical pulsar sources, ranging from ~ 100 - 1000 s, or even longer. The observed periods and period derivatives predict high magnetic fields, higher than the Schwinger field, when we consider conventional pulsar models of dipole-driven emission from neutron stars. #### Optimal galaxy magnification: generalized fundamental-plane residuals and number counts are all you need
 - **Authors:** Kun Xu, Ravi K. Sheth
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30133

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30133

 - **Abstract**
 Weak-lensing magnification complements cosmic shear through its effects on galaxy fluxes, sizes, and number counts. We derive the optimal magnification statistics from galaxy properties and show that the optimal measurement can be decomposed into an optimal mean statistic and total number counts. The optimal mean statistic can be constructed from the residual distribution of a generalized fundamental plane (GFP), $F(i,\boldsymbol{X})$, which predicts galaxy size or, equivalently, flux from surface brightness $i$ and additional lensing-invariant properties $\boldsymbol{X}$. The function $F$ can take any form that reduces the intrinsic scatter and thereby improves magnification precision. For a Gaussian residual distribution, the optimal mean statistic reduces to the inverse-variance-weighted residual, or simply the residual for constant scatter. Together with total number counts, this statistic captures all the magnification Fisher information under Poisson statistics, achieving the same precision as a full-likelihood analysis. This provides a simple and physically intuitive optimal compression of the full magnification likelihood, avoiding the need to model the potentially high-dimensional galaxy-property distribution, particularly when additional observables are incorporated to reduce the GFP scatter. Our results demonstrate that GFP residuals and number counts provide an optimal combination for weak-lensing magnification measurements in galaxy surveys.
