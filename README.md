 - **Abstract**
 We present a broadband, multiwavelength study of the interplay between the soft X-ray excess (SE), ultra-fast outflows (UFOs), and warm absorbers (WAs) in 21 active galactic nuclei (AGN; 0.1 <= z <= 0.4) from the SUBWAYS project. Our correlation analysis finds links between the amplification factor and WC temperature, WC optical depth and hardness ratio, and WC slope and optical depth with UV luminosity. We also find a tentative anticorrelation between WC optical depth and UFO outflow velocity.
#### How does a galaxy's quenching probability depend on its black hole's mass?
 - **Authors:** Antonio J. Porras-Valverde, John C. Forbes
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27061

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27061

 - **Abstract**
 We develop a parametric toy model to explore how black hole mass scatter $\sigma_{\rm BH}$ and the dependence of the quenching probability per unit time on black hole mass, parameterized as a power law with index $q$, jointly control the faint-end slope $\alpha$ of the stellar mass function of quenched central galaxies. By varying $\sigma_{\rm BH}$ and $q$, we identify two regimes. At low scatter, $\sigma_{\rm BH} \lesssim 0.4$ dex, $\alpha$ is strongly sensitive to $q$. Gradual quenching, represented by low $q$, produces shallow slopes, while sharp thresholds, with high $q$, produce steep mass functions, making $\alpha$ a powerful diagnostic of AGN feedback prescriptions. At large scatter, $\sigma_{\rm BH} \gtrsim 0.6$ dex, models with vastly different $q$ converge to similar quenched mass functions, because the intrinsic scatter erases the imprint of the feedback implementation. Comparing to cosmological hydrodynamical simulations and semianalytic models, only models with $\sigma_{\rm BH} \gtrsim 0.4$ dex match the observed faint-end slope of $\alpha \approx 0.7$. {\sc SAGE}, {\sc Dark Sage}, and {\sc COLIBRE}, with $\sigma_{\rm BH}$ of 2.0, 0.6, and 0.4 dex, respectively, reproduce this slope, whereas {\sc IllustrisTNG} and {\sc EAGLE}, with $\sigma_{\rm BH} \lesssim 0.2$ dex, produce steeper slopes. Current observational estimates of $\sigma_{\rm BH}$ allow a wide range of $q \gtrsim 0.8$, so the observed slope alone cannot identify the quenching prescription. If $\sigma_{\rm BH}$ is small, simultaneous measurements of $\alpha$ and $\sigma_{\rm BH}$ can distinguish between competing AGN feedback models.

#### Beyond static torques: Self-consistent migration and dynamical trapping of low-mass planets in dusty disks
 - **Authors:** Jose L. Gomez, Pablo Benitez-Llambay, Ximena S. Ramos, Octavio M. Guilera

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.27071

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.27071

 - **Abstract**
 The rapid inward migration of low-mass planets remains a primary theoretical challenge in planet formation. While recent studies show that the solid component of protoplanetary disks can exert strong positive torques to counteract the negative gas torques, traditional static fixed-orbit models fail to capture the fully coupled dynamics of a migrating planet. In this work, we present 2D high-resolution, two-fluid hydrodynamical simulations using a comoving reference frame to track the self-consistent orbital evolution of embedded planets across a comprehensive six-dimensional parameter space. By allowing the planet to dynamically respond to disk-exerted torques, we identify a wide diversity of migration regimes, involving inward/outward migration and oscillatory radial confinement. We also find that massive cores can overcome dust-induced barriers, experiencing dust-mediated runaway outward or inward migration. Conversely, in dust-rich environments continuous gas-dust momentum exchange combined with planet torques dynamically regenerates co-orbital dust asymmetries, creating robust dynamical attractors where super-Earths undergo stable, oscillatory confinement, effectively halting inward drift without requiring pre-existing pressure bumps or disk substructures. This self-induced trapping mechanism could be relevant in the outer disk, beyond the water-ice line in environments with a enhanced local dust-to-gas ratio, where icy pebbles are moderately coupled to the gas. In low-metallicity or highly diffusive disks, planets generally adopt monotonic inward decay, apart from cases with outward migration. Ultimately, the diversity of these self-consistent trajectories demonstrates that static torque approximations are insufficient. Evaluating fully coupled kinematic feedback is essential for accurately predicting planetary survival and the final architecture of planetary systems.

 - **Abstract**
 Blue supergiants (BSGs) are crucial to understanding the evolution of massive stars. Although distinguishing between pre- and post-red supergiant (RSG) phases is challenging, stellar pulsations provide valuable constraints on their masses and evolutionary status. We aim to estimate asteroseismic masses, compare them with spectroscopic and evolutionary mass estimates, and assess the evolutionary stage of the stars.  - **Abstract**

Gravity-mode pulsations in some sdBs offer a unique opportunity to extend recent successes in asteroseismic magnetic field detection in red giants.  - **Authors:** Connor Fredrick, Ryan K. Cole, Ryan Terrien, Suvrath Mahadevan, Scott A. Diddams

 - **Abstract**
 Precision spectroscopy underpins quantum sensing, atomic timekeeping, and the search for terrestrial-mass exoplanets. Finding an Earth-Sun analog requires fractional splitting of spectral features that rivals the precision of the best laboratory spectroscopy. This corresponds to resolution of a few parts in $10^5$ of a typical stellar spectral line, or a Doppler velocity precision of less than 10 cm s$^{-1}$. Only a few astrophysical spectrometers reach this regime, and only by dispersing starlight with large gratings and averaging thousands of lines. Here, we show that comparable precision can be achieved on a single solar line by leveraging techniques used in quantum sensors and optical clocks. Combining these techniques from quantum sensing with a thirty-meter-class telescope could enable our demonstrated precision on the nearest Sun-like stars in a few hours. Multiplexing such absolute frequency measurements across lines with different magnetic sensitivity and formation depth would reduce averaging time and help disentangle stellar granulation and activity from the Doppler signature of terrestrial exoplanets, providing new approaches to address some of the most demanding measurements in astrophysical spectroscopy.
#### Resonant three-mode coupling in asteroseismology I: Bifurcations, chaos, and DAV white-dwarf outbursts
 - **Authors:** Zhao Guo, Gordon I. Ogilvie
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28513

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28513

 - **Abstract**
 Resonant three-mode coupling is a fundamental nonlinear process in stellar oscillations and other wave systems. We investigate the global dynamics of a linearly overstable parent mode coupled to two damped daughter modes. The principal control parameters are frequency detuning $\Delta$ and daughter damping rate $\Gamma$, normalized by the parent growth rate. We combine high-resolution maps of the largest Lyapunov exponent in the $(\Gamma,\Delta)$ plane with linear stability analysis, Floquet calculations, and numerical continuation. The resulting parameter space includes stable equilibria, simple and period-doubled limit cycles, chaotic attractors, formally unbounded solutions, and higher-period `shrimp' islands within chaotic regions. Successive period doublings lead to chaos, while boundaries of isolated periodic windows exhibit type-I intermittency. At large $\Gamma$, the main bifurcation boundaries approach straight lines, indicating that the dynamics is governed primarily by $r=\Delta/(2\Gamma)$. We connect finite-$\Gamma$ bifurcations to an earlier one-dimensional return map. As $\epsilon\rightarrow0$, this map recovers the principal period-doubling sequence, the onset of chaos near $r\simeq0.38$, and the asymptotic positions of prominent periodic windows. We also examine the effects of unequal daughter damping rates. Applying these results to sporadic outbursts in cool DAV white dwarfs, we find that estimated $g$-mode growth rates, damping rates, and detunings place resonant triplets in both periodic and chaotic regimes. Thus, instability of the three-mode equilibrium need not produce a regular limit cycle: deterministic chaos and intermittency may contribute to irregular outburst amplitudes and recurrence times.

 - **Abstract**
 Dwarf galaxies are abundant and dark matter dominated, rendering them particularly relevant for testing a cosmological model dominated by a dark sector. Whether their density profiles agree with $\Lambda$CDM expectations has long been a point of contention, dubbed the cusp/core problem. While numerical simulations have suggested that the erasure of central dark matter cusps may arise from potential fluctuations in feedback-driven gas, whether the required fluctuation levels are achieved in actual galaxies has remained uncertain. By measuring the power spectra of neutral gas density contrasts in a sample of gas-rich dwarf galaxies, we show that the observed fluctuations match theoretical expectations when their impact is derived from a stochastic dynamical model.  - **Abstract**
 We investigate the dynamical stability of strongly magnetized supramassive neutron stars close to the mass-shedding limit using fully general-relativistic magnetohydrodynamic simulations in three spatial dimensions.
