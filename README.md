#### A Spectroscopic Census of Cluster White Dwarfs from DESI, LAMOST, and SDSS
 - **Authors:** David R. Miller, Jeremy Heyl, Huahui Yan, Chengyuan Li, Pier-Emmanuel Tremblay
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14166

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14166

 - **Abstract**
 Gaia has substantially expanded the sample of candidate white dwarfs (WDs) in open clusters, but many remain without spectroscopic characterization. We leverage the increasingly extensive archives of large public spectroscopic surveys to identify 62 candidate cluster WDs with available spectra from DESI, LAMOST, and SDSS. After discarding sources with very low S/N and unreliable host clusters, 44 candidates remain, including 37 non-magnetic DA WDs, of which 15 had not previously been characterized in the literature for cluster studies. We find that nine are consistent with single-star origins in their proposed host clusters, increasing the combined cluster DA initial--final mass relation (IFMR) sample to 132 WDs. All lie within $2\sigma$ of the best-fit IFMR from the most comprehensive recent cluster WD sample, suggesting these additions do not require recalibrating the empirical IFMR. One is a likely Hyades tidal-tail WD, providing further evidence that dynamical dispersal helps explain the long-standing WD deficit in the nearest cluster. We further compare WDs with spectra from multiple surveys. Among seven Praesepe WDs observed by both DESI and SDSS, DESI gives higher WD masses in six cases, with a mean difference of $0.067\,\mathrm{M}_\odot$, while the inferred progenitor masses differ by as much as $0.459\,\mathrm{M}_\odot$. More broadly, nine of ten single-star cluster DAs with DESI spectra lie above the empirical IFMR, with a mean final-mass residual of $0.06\pm0.04\,\mathrm{M}_\odot$. These results show that survey-dependent differences can strongly affect cluster WD parameters, and should be considered when combining heterogeneous spectroscopy in empirical IFMR studies.
#### Where Cluster Galaxies Reside and How Strongly They Lens
 - **Authors:** Isaque Dutra, Priyamvada Natarajan, Cian Roche
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14167

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14167

 - **Abstract**
 The efficiency of galaxy-galaxy strong lensing (GGSL) in clusters depends jointly on the inner mass distributions of member galaxies and their locations within the cluster potential. Observed GGSL probabilities exceed $\Lambda$CDM predictions by nearly an order of magnitude, but the relative roles of internal structure and environment remain unclear. We disentangle these effects by comparing HST strong-lensing clusters with TNG-Cluster analogs at similar lens redshifts, matched by their primary Einstein radius. For each member, we measure its lensing strength in isolation and when embedded in the cluster field. Members in cluster-critical regions are classified as Major or Minor Perturbers depending on whether they strongly lens in isolation; those in subcritical regions are classified as Intrinsic Lenses, Boosted Lenses, or Inert Members according to whether they lens independently, only with environmental assistance, or not at all. Observed clusters contain 33.7% of selected members in cluster-critical regions, compared with 21.6% in TNG-Cluster, while their Major-to-Minor ratios are similar. Outside these regions, observations contain substantially more Intrinsic and Boosted Lenses and fewer Inert Members, with Boosted Lens fractions of 25.8% and 13.5%, respectively. The discrepancy persists at fixed subhalo mass and after including line-of-sight structure. A mass-conserving contraction mimicking core-collapsed self-interacting dark matter largely recovers the conditional lensing fractions but not cluster-critical occupancy. The GGSL discrepancy therefore reflects both insufficient central concentration in simulated members and differences in their locations within the cluster lensing field.

We investigate parsec-scale asymmetric emission by modeling the squared visibilities and closure phases with a single circular Gaussian component plus a background point source for the central AGN, and searching for additional compact companions through grid-based binary models. #### The effect of triples on the Chemically Homogeneous Evolution Channel for producing Gravitational Wave Sources
 - **Authors:** Silvia A. Popa, Selma E. de Mink, Stephen Justham, Jakob Stegmann, Alejandro Vigna-Gomez, Lieke A. C. van Son
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14353

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.14353

 - **Abstract**
 Despite the growing number of gravitational-wave (GW) detections, the formation of merging binary black holes (BBHs) is still debated. One of the proposed formation channels is Chemically Homogeneous Evolution (CHE), which makes distinct predictions for the properties of the resulting black holes (BHs): high masses ($\gtrsim35\, M_\odot$) and large, orbitally-aligned spins. These predictions are based on studies that consider this channel only in isolated binaries. However, most massive stars reside in triples. We investigate how a tertiary affects the CHE channel. We use detailed binary models computed with MESA for the inner CHE systems coupled with analytical prescriptions for the hierarchical triple dynamics. Specifically, we study the von Zeipel-Lidov-Kozai mechanism that can accelerate the merger of the inner BBHs through high-eccentricity excitations. We show that a tertiary companion enables mergers at higher metallicities, where the classical binary CHE channel does not operate. At $Z_\odot/3$, triples produce BHs with lower masses (around $15\,M_\odot$) and lower spins (around 0.25) compared to the low-metallicity classical scenario. We further expect triple dynamics to lead to spin-orbit misalignment and potential residual eccentricities at merger. In triples at $Z_\odot/3$, half of the mergers occur within 1 Gyr. Moreover, 10% of the population leads to prompt mergers within 1 Myr (1% within 1 kyr) that are of potential interest for electromagnetic counterparts. We conclude that the triple CHE channel is promising as (i) it may contribute more substantially to the local merger rate than had been expected for the classical CHE channel due to the shorter delay times and effectiveness at higher metallicities and (ii) produces mergers with properties that more closely resemble the lower masses and lower spins inferred for most of the observed GW merger population.

We present an X-ray, optical, and radio study of GS 1843+00 during this outburst, focusing on the evolution of the neutron star emission geometry, Be-disk connection, and possible outflow activity.  - **Authors:** Sheng-Lun Xie, Yi Zhao, Wen-Long Zhang, Chen-Wei Wang, Yun-Wei Yu, Shao-Lin Xiong
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.14581

 - **Abstract**
 Magnetar bursts are commonly attributed to magnetic energy release, yet the physically relevant dissipation unit has remained uncertain. Here we argue that this unit is not the burst as conventionally defined, but the short substructures within bursts, namely magnetar X-ray flares (MXFs). Using 789 MXFs from SGR~J1935+2154, we show that their waiting times separate into two components, interpreted as reflecting clustered intra-episode events and longer inter-episode intervals, that their proxy emission measure and temperature exhibit distinct rising and declining trends, and that their energy release scale (ERS) proxy distribution has a high-end slope in the same statistical regime as solar and stellar flares. We infer that magnetar active episodes are global unloading states assembled from many elementary flares. Magnetar X-ray flares are therefore the physically relevant units for magnetar outburst statistics and for connecting magnetar high-energy activity to other impulsive phenomena.

 - **Abstract**
 The flux variability of Active Galactic Nuclei (AGNs) is commonly modeled using the damped random walk (DRW), but increasingly rich multiband time-domain surveys motivate more flexible stochastic models that characterize variability beyond a single timescale and exploit dependence across bands.  - **Abstract**

These include tidal disruption events (TDEs), changing-look active galactic nuclei, Bowen fluorescence flares (BFFs) and ambiguous nuclear transients, alongside supernovae that appear nuclear but are unrelated to the SMBH. Reliable classification requires spectroscopy, which will not scale to the alert volumes of the Legacy Survey of Space and Time (LSST). #### Differential Luminosity Response of Balmer-Line Profiles in Galactic B/A Luminous Stars
 - **Authors:** N. L. Vaidman, S. A. Khokhlov, Sh. T. Nurmakhametova, N. A. Litvinov, A. T. Agishev, D. A. Alimbetova
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18810

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18810

 - **Abstract**
 We investigate the differential luminosity response of the optical Balmer series from H8 to H-alpha in Galactic B5-A5 luminous stars using homogeneous measurements of central depth, FWHM, and equivalent width at a resolving power of approximately 12,000. Direct line-to-line comparisons show a systematic change in the central-depth response toward the lower-order transitions. For the primary 72-star luminosity sample, the fitted luminosity coefficients show an overall positive trend with upper-level quantum number, with a point-estimate gradient of dq/dn = 0.0419. A joint Monte Carlo analysis with 10,000 realisations gives a median dq/dn = 0.0387 and a 95 percent interval from 0.0253 to 0.0532, with no non-positive gradients obtained. The higher-order H8-H-delta coefficients form a statistically unresolved plateau, followed by a well-supported increase in response magnitude from H-delta toward H-alpha. The result is robust to the adopted effective-temperature scale. The FWHM coefficients show no comparable Balmer-order progression, and the directly fitted profile-shape response is consistent with zero from H8 through H-beta. For H-alpha, the full logarithmic sample is not formally significant, whereas removal of the three remaining strongly filled-core profiles yields a significantly negative shape response. The observed behaviour is qualitatively compatible with the systematic variation of pressure broadening across the Balmer series, while the enhanced H-alpha response may additionally reflect non-LTE and wind-related effects.
