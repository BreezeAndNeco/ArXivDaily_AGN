#### The impact of nonlinear Landau damping on cosmic ray streaming and the role of external turbulence
 - **Authors:** Rouven Lemmerz, Mohamad Shalaby, Ellen Zweibel, Christoph Pfrommer, Timon Thomas
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); Astrophysics of Galaxies (astro-ph.GA); Plasma Physics (physics.plasm-ph)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20695

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20695

 - **Abstract**
 Cosmic rays (CRs) streaming faster than the Alfvén speed generate Alfvén waves that scatter them. The saturated wave amplitude is regulated by a damping mechanism. This damping controls the CR scattering rate and drift velocity, and thus the rate at which CRs transfer momentum and energy to the ambient plasma, which sets the strength of CR feedback. In the ionized interstellar medium, nonlinear Landau damping (NLLD) is commonly invoked as the dominant damping mechanism for Alfvén waves. We test this assumption with fluid-PIC simulations in which thermal ions and electrons are modeled as fluids with optional Landau closures, allowing us to compare otherwise identical runs with and without NLLD. When waves are self-generated by monoenergetic CRs, runs with and without Landau closures are indistinguishable: NLLD is inefficient and the CRs isotropize in the wave frame. Once high-amplitude Alfvén waves mimicking external turbulence are seeded, NLLD curbs wave growth and equalizes forward- and backward-propagating waves. We show that the saturated drift velocity, which falls below the Alfvén speed, can be accurately predicted from the wave spectra alone. If the CR distribution is undersampled, a numerical instability corrupts the largest resonant scales: unphysical, unresolved pitch-angle gradients resonantly generate backward-propagating waves, driving an inverse cascade that should be absent from purely streaming-driven waves. While power-law CR distributions may permit stronger NLLD, our results indicate that NLLD is unlikely to be an intrinsic saturation mechanism of the streaming instability; instead, its effectiveness hinges on the interplay of streaming-generated modes with those of external origin.

However, their black-hole (BH) masses, BH-to-stellar mass ratios, cold-gas reservoirs, and depletion times remain uncertain. The recent discovery of low-redshift LRDs at $z\simeq0.1 - 0.9$ creates a decisive opportunity to access their low-$J$ CO lines for measuring molecular gas reservoirs, and the nearest systems can in principle be spatially resolved. We use J1025+1402 ("The Egg") at $z=0.1007$, the best-studied local LRD, as a benchmark to explore the scientific opportunities with ALMA2040. Assuming a molecular gas mass of $10^9\,M_\odot$, the CO(2-1) detectability strongly depends on the assumed CO-to-H$_2$ conversion ($\alpha_{\rm CO}$). We conclude that ALMA2040 has the potential to directly measure the gas masses, depletion times, and excitation of local LRDs, while the CO-bright subset may ultimately yield dynamical constraints on the mass of their central black holes.

 - **Authors:** Damir Gasymov, Ivan Yu. Katkov, Eva K. Grebel, Anna Pasquali, Evgenii V. Rubtsov, Katarina Kraljic, Igor V. Chilingarian, Kirill A. Grishin, Anna S. Saburova

 - **Abstract**
 Large-scale stellar counter-rotation, in which two coplanar stellar components rotate in opposite directions, is usually interpreted as a signature of external gas accretion. Such systems are most often found in relatively sparse environments, where cold gas can be acquired and remain dynamically undisturbed long enough to settle into a disk. It is less clear whether this channel can operate in massive central galaxies, whose group or cluster environments can heat, strip, or perturb cold gas. Here we present NGC 5717, one of the most massive galaxies known to host a counter-rotating stellar disk and the first identified case in a brightest group galaxy. Using integral-field spectroscopy from SDSS-IV Mapping Nearby Galaxies at Apache Point Observatory (MaNGA), together with CFHT imaging, ancillary HI data, and SDSS/DESI spectroscopy of the surrounding group, we show that the galaxy contains a dynamically cold counter-rotating disk embedded in an old dynamically hot elliptical component. The disk is young ($\sim 1.3$ Gyr), metal-poor ([Fe/H] $\sim -0.7$ dex), extended ($h \sim 2.4$ kpc), and massive ($\log M_\star/M_\odot \sim 9.9$); the associated ionized gas co-rotates with it, while the available HI data indicate no significant remaining reservoir of neutral atomic gas ($\log M_{\mathrm{HI}}/M_\odot \lesssim 9$). These properties support an in situ origin of the secondary component from externally accreted gas. The chemical evolution model favors gas accretion from a cosmic filament, which is consistent with the surrounding cosmic-web geometry, and indicates highly efficient burst-like star formation with substantial metal-enriched outflows. NGC 5717 may therefore represent a rare example of disk growth in a massive central galaxy, enabled by external cold-gas accretion during an earlier phase of the group assembly history.
#### How Little Red Dots Turn Blue: Evolution of Kelvin-Helmholtz Contracting Black Hole Envelopes and Observational Diagnostics
 - **Authors:** Zihao Wang, Kohei Inayoshi

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20709

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20709

 - **Abstract**
 James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) observations have revealed compact, red-colored, broad-line emitters known as little red dots (LRDs), while a bluer counterpart population has been referred to as little blue dots (LBDs). Their physical connection remains unclear: they may represent different evolutionary stages of the same phenomenon, distinct populations, or different phases of the broader evolution of early galaxies and their black holes (BHs). We propose a model in which a rapidly growing BH is embedded in a geometrically thick gaseous envelope accompanied by concurrent nuclear star formation. The model reproduces the observed spectral energy distributions (SEDs) of LRDs and LBDs, and naturally connects the two phases through Kelvin-Helmholtz contraction of the envelope and exposure of the nuclear accretion disk, as the gas supply declines due to external stellar activity or feedback. We predict the corresponding evolutionary tracks in the UV-optical slope ($\beta_{\rm UV}$-$\beta_{\rm opt}$) plane which bridge the apparent gap between LRDs and LBDs. These evolutionary tracks start from the LRD regime and consistently exhibit a red excursion that extends toward $(\beta_{\rm UV},\beta_{\rm opt})\sim(2,-1)$ before turning toward the LBD regime. This feature is largely insensitive to variations in the model parameters that govern the envelope structure, providing a robust signature of the transitional population. The transition lasts $\sim5$-$50\,\mathrm{Myr}$, depending primarily on the BH feedback efficiency and the gas-retention efficiency of the envelope. The abundance ratio of transitional systems to LRDs therefore provides a constraint on BH feedback and gas retention during the rapid growth of early supermassive BHs.

 - **Abstract**
 The extended HI rotation curves of galaxies are one of the most powerful tools for studying galaxy evolution and for testing different dark matter and modified gravity models. However, their sample sizes have historically been limited to a few hundred galaxies, mostly due to the survey speed of radio interferometers. Here, we present a first data release of HI kinematic models from the Apertif Wide-area Extragalactic Survey (AWES). Starting from 1924 galaxies detected in HI, we constructed 568 reliable kinematic models, which provide rotation curves, velocity dispersion profiles, surface density profiles, and HI disk geometries.  - **Abstract**

Many mitigation models describe coupling as a linear combination of uncoupled signals re-radiated from neighboring feeds, but data suggest additional mechanisms. Inspired by antenna scattering theory, we decompose coupling into an antenna mode, constructed from translated isolated responses and expected to be removable by linear combination models, and a structural mode, the remainder.
