 - **Abstract**
 I performed photometric observations of the poorly studied cataclysmic variable MASTER OT J232100.42+494614.0 and discovered eclipses. I observed nine eclipses over two groups of closely spaced nights, separated by a 12-month interval. I precisely calculated the orbital period of the system to be Porb=0.212371533+\-0.000000069 days. For the eclipses, I created an ephemeris that is accurate for a long time span and suitable for analysing changes in the orbital period. The star's brightness outside of the eclipses varied between 15.61+\-0.02 and 18.04+\-0.05 mag. As the brightness decreased, the eclipses became more profound whereas its width remained unchanged. The average depth of eclipses was 1.36+\-0.03 mag, and the average width at half-depth was 0.0512(15) phases. I calculated the possible inclination of the orbit to be between 72.8 and 76.3 degrees, and the range of the average absolute V-band magnitude of the disc to be between 6.37+\-0.35 and 6.68+\-0.35 mag. Although the light curve of MASTER OT J232100.42+494614.0 from the ZTF survey showed insufficiently large outbursts with amplitudes less than 2.5 mag, it should be classified as a dwarf nova, as the average disc brightness and mass transfer rate are below the threshold for thermal instability.

 - **Abstract**
 Understanding how supermassive black holes (SMBHs) grow and regulate the growth of their host galaxies requires tracing the cold molecular interstellar medium (ISM) throughout the full AGN feeding-feedback cycle. Molecular gas constitutes both the primary fuel for star formation and black-hole accretion and one of the ISM phases most directly affected by AGN-driven radiation, winds, and radio jets. Over the past decade, ALMA has resolved circumnuclear molecular discs, rings, spirals, tori, and jet-driven outflows on scales of tens of parsecs in nearby AGN. These studies have demonstrated that combining spatially resolved gas kinematics with multi-line diagnostics is essential for disentangling the physical processes underlying the observed gas conditions. However, our current picture remains largely based on a limited number of exceptionally nearby systems. Most local AGN have instead been observed with ALMA in a single transition at moderate or low angular resolution, preventing a systematic characterisation of both the dynamics and physical state of the gas. Moreover, existing studies have largely overlooked kinetic-mode AGN hosted by massive early-type galaxies, despite their central importance for understanding maintenance-mode feedback. In this White Paper, we argue that ALMA2040 should transform these detailed case studies into a statistical framework. A $\sim10\times$ improvement in line sensitivity relative to the current ALMA, together with fast mosaicking, ultra-wide instantaneous bandwidth, and longer baselines (resolving up to $\sim9$ mas at 230 GHz), will enable homogeneous, multi-line mapping of the molecular ISM on giant molecular clouds scales across statistically significant samples of both radiative- and kinetic-mode AGN out to $z\lesssim0.1$.

 - **Abstract**
 How does a star's birth environment set the mass, angular momentum and chemical complexity delivered to its planet-forming disk, and thereby whether it builds planets, brown dwarfs or stellar companions? This requires following material from the cloud to the sub-au to few-au scales where infalling gas, magnetized winds and the disk interact, across all star-forming environments. At frequencies where young disks are optically thin, current facilities, even ALMA after its Wideband Sensitivity Upgrade, fall short by a factor of a few in angular resolution and an order of magnitude in sensitivity. Likewise, resolving how disks around distant high-mass stars fragment into tight multiples requires a linear resolution of a few to a few tens of au, currently out of reach. Yet the disks characterized so far lie almost exclusively in nearby, isolated clouds at near-solar metallicity, although the environment (radiation field, ionization rate, gas and stellar density, pressure and metallicity) varies by orders of magnitude across the Galaxy. Most stars, probably including the Sun, form in clustered regions at kpc distances, and the widest metallicity range lies farther still, in the outer Galaxy, the Central Molecular Zone and the Magellanic Clouds. With three times finer angular resolution and ten times the sensitivity, ALMA2040 will follow the path of material from cloud to disk across the full stellar-mass spectrum and, for the first time, measure how this path changes with environment: resolving the sub-au infall-impact and wind-launching zones nearby, disk fragmentation and the onset of multiplicity in distant high-mass regions, and disk formation at a few tenths of solar metallicity. It will thereby reveal the dust-obscured, embedded origins of planetary systems that optical and infrared facilities, including the ELT, see only once they emerge, and show how the birth environment shapes them.

 - **Abstract**
 We present a multi-wavelength analysis of the nearby spiral galaxy NGC 6946, combining optical IFS, infrared, and radio observations. We aim to characterise both the integrated and spatially resolved properties of the stellar populations and ISM, and to interpret them in the context of bar-driven galaxy evolution. We used the SED fitting code SINOPSIS to derive stellar population properties, and different prescriptions to derive ISM properties including atomic and molecular gas masses and the dust mass,temperature, and emissivity index. We find that the central 2.6 kpc hosts $40\%$ of the stellar mass, as well as the bulk of the molecular gas and dust, and has sustained enhanced star formation over cosmic time. We also find indications consistent with a young stellar bar. The current decline in star formation despite large gas reservoirs suggests recent bar-driven quenching.

 - **Authors:** Michael P. Grehan, Christopher Thompson, Bart Ripperda, James R. Beattie

 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22653

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22653

 - **Abstract**
 Magnetic reconnection around neutron stars (NSs) forms small-scale currents while transferring energy to electron-positron pairs. These dissipating currents cover a wide range of scales, potentially leaving an imprint on time-variable X-ray bursts and pulsed radio emission. We present a reduced, planar model of internal reconnection in a magnetic flux tube with relativistic magnetization, finite resistivity, and strong guide field. Comparing with realistic NS parameters, this model represents the dynamics of a slender magnetic flux tube that is much thinner than the stellar radius. The seed current profile is smooth and does not spontaneously reconnect; instead, a discrete and localized current perturbation is introduced, representing e.g. localized energy release from a NS crust or magnetosphere. Three high-resolution relativistic magnetohydrodynamics simulations are described: one has uniform resistivity and Lundquist number such that reconnection proceeds in the plasmoid-mediated regime; the other two account for the feedback of charge starvation on the resistivity in current layers, where density is depleted; we provide a more general explanation for this depletion. All simulations produce a few dominant magnetic X-points and a secondary Kelvin-Helmholtz instability that excites high-wavenumber currents. We quantify the reconnection rate, the intermittency of dissipation, and the time profile of current spikes. We further present three-dimensional simulations confirming a limited growth of the ideal kink mode in the strong guide field regime. Applications are discussed, including to radio pulse microstructure and current damping below the magnetar surface during an X-ray flare, leading to mass loss and X-ray afterglow.

 - **Authors:** Brad N. Barlow, Bennett C. Kirby

 - **Abstract**
 The Hertzsprung-Russell diagram is one of the most important tools in stellar astrophysics. In its modern, empirical form as the Gaia color-magnitude diagram (CMD), each star is represented with a point determined from its time-averaged brightness and color. However, many stars are intrinsically variable, and the fixed-point representation ignores the variety of ways in which a star can vary. As a system pulsates, eclipses, is irradiated, erupts, or otherwise varies, it moves through the CMD along a trajectory whose size, slope, and shape are determined by the physics driving the brightness and color variability. Here we exploit the near-simultaneous, three-band, multi-epoch photometry (MEP) released for ~11.7 million sources in Gaia DR3 to construct a variability-encoded CMD (VECMD). In this updated take on the traditional CMD, each star is represented not by a point but by a linear segment approximating the path it traces in absolute magnitude and color as it varies. Because these trajectories are built from the raw epoch data, they are agnostic to period and do not require phase-folding. We show that radial-mode pulsators trace looped, Lissajous-like trajectories that can encode the phase lag between radius and temperature. The Hertzsprung bump is even visible in the CMD loops of some Cepheids. Variable binary systems can trace tight color-correlated lines and/or hooked paths. Accreting and dust-obscured systems trace long, high-amplitude tracks aligned with the outburst or reddening vector. Distinct regions of the VECMD are dominated by distinct trajectory morphologies. We argue that these trajectories are period-independent fingerprints well suited to machine learning classification, which we will pursue on the much larger epoch-photometry sample expected in Gaia DR4. Finally, we present a free app called IVECMD for finding, classifying, and analyzing variable stars in the VECMD.

 - **Abstract**
 The standard magnetic braking (MB) model has long failed to explain key properties of low-mass X-ray binaries (LMXBs)---including their accretion rates, the formation of ultracompact binaries, and orbital expansion---casting doubt on binary evolution and gravitational-wave progenitor predictions. Phenomenological modifications exist, but a unified, physically grounded mechanism has been missing.
