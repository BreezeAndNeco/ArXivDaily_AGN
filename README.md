#### General-purpose equations of state for hadron-quark matter: first application to accretion-induced phase transitions in neutron stars
 - **Authors:** János Takátsy, Henrique Gieg, Tim Dietrich
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc); High Energy Physics - Phenomenology (hep-ph); Nuclear Theory (nucl-th)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30233

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30233

 - **Abstract**
 Strong first-order phase transitions in the cores of neutron stars (NSs) can have profound implications for astrophysical observables, however, their existence remains an open question. Since most NSs form in binary systems, they are expected to experience prolonged episodes of mass accretion that gradually increase their central density and can potentially trigger such a transition. This might, in turn, generate observable multi-messenger signals, as suggested by previous studies. Motivated by this prospect, we generate a new set of equations of state for hadron--quark matter with first-order phase transitions of various strengths, including neutrino absorption opacities in the quark phase as well. We then perform general-relativistic radiation hydrodynamics simulations of NSs undergoing accretion-induced phase transitions. In contrast to previous findings, our results indicate that these systems do not produce unbound ejecta for the models considered, making accretion-induced phase transitions unlikely sources of observable electromagnetic transients. However, we find that transitions are accompanied by intense neutrino emission, with peak luminosities reaching $\sim10^{52}~\mathrm{erg/s}$ for the strongest transition considered. To facilitate future numerical studies, we make the family of equations of state and neutrino opacities used in this work publicly available, enabling the systematic exploration of the role of the phase transition strength.

 - **Abstract**
 Understanding the influence of cloud-circulation feedback on planetary climates is crucial to interpreting solar system and extrasolar planet observations. Hot Jupiter phase curve observations have reported westward optical phase curve offsets and weak absorption features, which may be explained by the presence of clouds. JWST observations have also revealed tentative silicate cloud absorption features in emission and transmission spectroscopy. While a number of 3D atmospheric dynamics models of hot Jupiters and microphysical models describing cloud formation exist, few combine these two approaches. To help elucidate the impact of clouds on hot Jupiters, we introduce a post-processing scheme with the 3D GCM ADAM with SPARC (formerly SPARC/MITgcm), the 1D microphysical cloud model CARMA, and the radiative transfer code PICASO. We first simulate the cloud-free atmosphere of WASP-43b with ADAM, then incorporate resulting thermal profiles into CARMA to simulate WASP-43b's cloud structure, and finally generate phase-dependent emission spectra from our results with PICASO. By comparing to JWST observations, we infer that WASP-43b has a cloudy nightside, a mostly clear morning and evening limb with patchy clouds, and a clear dayside at the level of the photosphere. Our microphysical model predicts the condensation of five cloud species, including silicates, but overestimates the cloud opacity at all phases compared to observations, requiring additional modeling to explain. The results from this post-processing setup are promising for more meaningful characterization of WASP-43b's atmosphere, and will serve as a comparison baseline for our upcoming indirect coupling of both models, as well as for other future studies.

 - **Abstract**
 The ionizing radiation that galaxies release into their surroundings depends on whether efficient Lyman continuum (LyC) escape coincides with strong photon production. Stellar feedback can open escape paths as the source population fades, linking the escaping radiation to a galaxy's recent history. We investigate this timing in thirteen high-redshift (z > 3) target haloes from the THESAN-ZOOM radiation-hydrodynamic simulations, post-processed with COLT radiative transfer and sampled at ~10 Myr intervals. The clearest delayed relationship links $\text{SFR}_{10}/\text{SFR}_{100}$ to the LyC escape fraction. Selecting the nearest positive cross-correlation peak gives typical lags of ~16 Myr and peak correlations of 0.4-0.6 in massive haloes, with longer and less coherent delays towards lower masses. The fitted delays scale approximately as $M_\star^{-0.19}$ and $M_\text{halo}^{-0.27}$, although the shortest lags approach the native snapshot spacing. Recurrent variations span multiple amplitude-dependent timescales, consistent with repeated episodes of star formation and feedback. Recent star formation closely tracks both intrinsic and escaping photon rates, while the escape fraction reflects the evolving gas transparency. The H$\alpha$-to-UV luminosity ratio retains a delayed association with escape, offering an observable connection between burst phase and leakage. High transparency during a fading phase can nevertheless yield little escaping radiation. The temporal overlap of photon production and transmission therefore shapes both the amount and timing of escaping radiation, informing the interpretation of observed leakers and their contribution to cosmic reionization.
#### Density-split analyses of the pairwise kinetic Sunyaev-Zeldovich effect
 - **Authors:** Finn A. Roper, Aritra K. Gon, Yan-Chuan Cai, John A. Peacock
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30267

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30267

 - **Abstract**
 We consider the dependence of the kinetic Sunyaev-Zeldovich (kSZ) effect on the environments of foreground cosmological structures. Standard pairwise kSZ estimators exploit the fact that galaxies below separations of $\sim 200\,h^{-1}{\rm M}_\odot$ fall towards each other on average, yielding a negative mean pairwise kSZ signal. However, this signal is the residual from a near-cancellation between outflows from underdense regions and infall towards overdensities giving a large variance in the pairwise velocity distribution and hence a low signal-to-noise ratio for the mean kSZ signal. This effect is especially marked at the largest scales, where the pairwise velocity distribution becomes more symmetric about zero. We show that splitting galaxies according to their local overdensity allows the convergence and divergence flows to be separated, yielding pairwise kSZ signals of opposing sign, with a reduction in variance for each subset. We use simulations to perform a detailed analysis of this phenomenon, comparing the cosmological information content in the global pairwise kSZ signal with our new density-split pairwise kSZ estimator. We consider galaxy catalogues comparable to datasets from the Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument, together with cosmic microwave background instrumental noise characteristic of recent and planned ground-based experiments. We predict that the density-split pairwise kSZ will increase the significance of detection by only a modest factor; nevertheless, we expect improvements by factors of respectively 11 and 4 in constraining $\sigma_8$ and $\Omega_{\rm m}$ when using the combination of Dark Energy Spectroscopic Instrument and Simons Observatory Large Aperture Telescope surveys.

 - **Abstract**
 The M-sigma correlation of inactive galaxies plays a crucial role in estimating the black hole mass (MBH) in active galactic nuclei (AGN): 1) it is used to calibrate the reverberation mapping (RM) method; 2) the RM is the basis of the single epoch method, the most-used technique to determine MBH of broad-line AGN; and 3) in narrow-line AGN, MBH is often directly inferred from the M-sigma correlation. The main goal of this work is to test whether the M-sigma relation calibrated from inactive galaxies can be reliably applied to a local AGN population spanning Seyfert 1, Seyfert 2, and hidden broad-line region galaxies (HBLRs), and whether the resulting AGN relation has sufficient predictive power to be used as an indirect MBH estimator. To investigate this hypothesis, we used the 12 micron AGN sample, a local representative and largely unbiased sample. Since a substantial fraction of this sample has good-quality NuSTAR data, we estimated their MBH with the X-ray scaling method, which works for any AGN accreting at moderate or high level. We find that the M-sigma correlation of the 12 micron AGN sample is different from the one obtained with inactive galaxies and has a substantial scatter. Therefore, its predictive power for MBH estimates is fairly limited. We also find that the MBH-Mgal correlation based on the 12 micron AGN sample is consistent with the analogous correlation obtained from a large sample of nearby type 1 AGN and inconsistent with that of inactive galaxies. Finally, we find suggestive evidence that Seyfert 1 and HBLRs have similar and systematically higher Eddington ratios than Seyfert 2 galaxies.

 - **Abstract**
 The early evolutionary stages of some active regions (ARs) are analysed with the aim of resolving one of the paramount-importance problems of heliophysics: what plays a primary, active role in the formation of active regions and sunspots---magnetic fields or motion of solar plasma? In particular, the first view underlies the popular model of rising $\Omega$-shaped, twisted flux-tube loop of strong magnetic field.  - **Abstract**
 We examine the long-term evolution of localized eccentricity perturbations in a softened Laplace-Lagrange chain. The secular coupling between mass elements is long-ranged, suggesting that any disturbance imposed at a single radius should disperse rapidly across the chain. We show that this expectation fails once the secular interaction is regularized by a finite softening length $\beta$: while broad eccentricity profiles propagate smoothly, as textbook secular theory anticipates, narrow profiles remain confined to their launching site for millions of secular periods. For a wide field-of-view planar polarimeter, GRBs arrive off axis, where the Klein-Nishina projection adds harmonics to the ASAD. Muleri (2014) described this projection and evaluated it in a low-energy expansion for X-rays. We evaluate it numerically without approximation over the whole prompt-emission energy band for any incidence and polarization angles, quantify the off-axis harmonics a single modulation factor cannot capture, and verify the model against GEANT4 simulations of an ideal polarimeter. We also include the effect of the detection threshold on the ASAD. With POLAR as the case study, we present two modes that build the full polarization response with at least 95 percent fewer Monte Carlo simulations. Mode 1 takes only the unpolarized simulation per energy and recovers the polarization measured with POLAR's full GEANT4 responses for all but the brightest GRBs, where it underestimates the polarization degree. Mode 2 adds two polarized simulations per energy at distinct angles and reproduces the simulated ASADs to their statistical noise and the polarization of every GRB tested. Both modes are released as open-source software for any planar Compton polarimeter.
#### Granulation-driven spectral irradiance variability of Sun-like stars
 - **Authors:** N.-E. Nèmec, G. Kopp, V. Witzke, N. M. Kostogryz
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31561

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31561

 - **Abstract**
 Granulation,caused by convection in low-mass stars like the Sun, introduces small amplitude variations in the stellar brightness on timescales of minutes to hours. Often referred to as flicker or microvariability this signal can impede detecting Earth-sized planets around Sun-like stars via stellar transits. Stellar flicker can also introduce uncertainties in determining stellar fundamental parameters and cause errors of up to 10% in the radius determination of transiting exoplanets. Both sources of uncertainties will propagate into planetary transmission spectra and resulting atmospheric retrievals. We present a method for modelling stellar flicker that enables scaling the flicker of Sun-like stars between different wavelengths and passbands to facilitate better solar-stellar comparisons. We use the 3D radiative magnetohydrodynamic code MURaM to simulate the granulation pattern on a Sun-like star and employ the radiative transfer code MPS-ATLAS for the spectral synthesis of the MURaM output, producing lightcurves of the granulation signal in various wavelengths and at different disc positions. Using these synthetic lightcurves, we establish scaling relations in the time and frequency domain between different wavelengths and passbands routinely used in stellar observations, such as the Kepler and TESS space telescopes. We find that the variability caused by granulation is very well correlated across wavelengths from 300 to 6000 nm, enabling scalings from one wavelength regime to another. We present the scaling sensitivities in different passbands and wavelength regimes that can be used to facilitate solar-stellar comparisons and help estimate stellar flicker in observations using photometric passbands and low-resolution transmission spectra.
#### Charged fluid tori with an approximate magnetic self-field around magnetized Schwarzschild black holes: A semi-analytic approach
 - **Authors:** Abylaikhan Tlemissov, Jiri Kovar
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
High Energy Astrophysical Phenomena (astro-ph.HE); General Relativity and Quantum Cosmology (gr-qc)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31577

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31577

 - **Abstract**
 We study toroidal structures formed by a charged, non-self-gravitating perfect fluid orbiting a Schwarzschild black hole immersed in a poloidal magnetic field. To obtain analytic and semi-analytic solutions, we assume a cold, rest-mass-dominated fluid with uniform specific charge and zero conductivity, so the current is purely convective. This regime is formally opposite to the ideal GRMHD limit. The total magnetic field comprises a prescribed asymptotically uniform external component and an approximate self-field associated with the azimuthal current in the torus. We represent the latter by a regularized dipole approximation to the field of an effective circular current loop. Neglecting the self-field, the method of characteristics yields a general implicit solution of the integrability condition for a function parametrizing the specific-angular-momentum profile. A separately obtained cylindrically dependent class recovers a solution from our earlier work. For the radially dependent class, we integrate the Euler equations, obtain the enthalpy potential, and derive local conditions for equatorially centered tori. We then examine the field-free limit, both magnetic components separately, and their superposition. The external field supports closed tori within analytically derived parameter bounds, whereas no such equilibrium is found for the isolated approximate self-field within the numerically explored domain. In a representative combined-field sequence, the radial extent varies nonmonotonically with relative self-field strength: it increases for weak self-fields and decreases as the self-field becomes comparable to the external field. The results show how an approximate magnetic self-field modifies the morphology and domain of existence of charged-fluid tori.
#### Magnetic Activity as a Driver of Low-gravity-like Spectral Morphology in Late-M Dwarfs
 - **Authors:** Chia-Lung Lin, Daniel Apai, Benjamin V. Rackham, Prajwal Niraula, Mark S. Giampapa, Ana Glidden, Julien de Wit, Valeriy Vasilyev, Nadiia Kostogryz, Alexander Shapiro
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Solar and Stellar Astrophysics (astro-ph.SR)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31647

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31647

 - **Abstract**
 We present a systematic investigation of the connection between magnetic activity and the low-surface-gravity-like near-infrared spectral morphology of late-M dwarfs. Our sample includes TRAPPIST-1 observed with JWST NIRISS/SOSS and 22 late-M dwarfs observed with IRTF/SpeX, together with flare observations from K2, TESS, JWST NIRISS/SOSS, and literature-reported X-ray luminosities. Using one-component PHOENIX/NewEra spectral modeling, we find that all targets are better reproduced by lower-surface-gravity model spectra than by models with the expected high gravities of field late-M dwarfs. The retrieved surface gravities moderately correlate with independently measured gravity-sensitive spectral indices, with the strongest trends seen for FeH$_z$ at 0.998~$\mu$m and FeH$_J$ at 1.2~$\mu$m, both showing statistically significant Pearson and Spearman correlations with $p<0.005$. They also show strong anti-correlations with flare luminosity and X-ray luminosity: for both relations, Pearson and Spearman coefficients are $<-0.9$, with $p \leq 1.0\times10^{-4}$, indicating that more active stars tend to have spectra favoring lower-gravity morphologies. Time-series spectra of TRAPPIST-1 during two large flares show no clear short-timescale variations in selected photospheric indices, suggesting that persistent small-scale magnetic activity may play a more important role than large flares in modifying the photospheric temperature--pressure structure. A simplified radiative-transfer experiment further shows that activity-associated heating can produce lower-gravity-like spectral features even when the input surface gravity is fixed. Our results suggest that underestimated surface gravities in late-M dwarfs may reflect atmospheres modified by frequent, small-scale magnetic activity, such as microflares, producing emergent spectra that mimic lower-gravity morphology.
#### Sound-Horizon-Agnostic Forecast for Constraints on $H_0$ and $Σm_ν$ from BAO, CMB Lensing, Supernovae and LSST-like Galaxy Lensing
 - **Authors:** Seyed Hamidreza Mirpoorian, Helena García Escudero, Levon Pogosian, Amy Wayland, David Alonso
 - **Subjects:** Subjects:
Cosmology and Nongalactic Astrophysics (astro-ph.CO)
 - **Arxiv link:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31660

 - **Pdf link:** https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31660

 - **Abstract**
 We present a sound-horizon-agnostic forecast for constraints on the Hubble constant $H_0$ and the sum of the neutrino masses $\Sigma m_\nu$ from a combination of LSST-like galaxy weak lensing, Simons-Observatory-like CMB lensing, the completed DESI BAO program, and expanded supernova samples. Treating the sound horizon at baryon drag $r_d$ as a free parameter, the method provides a consistency test of $\Lambda$CDM without relying on the standard early-Universe calibration of $r_d$. The analysis incorporates realistic galaxy weak-lensing systematics, explicitly marginalizing over photometric-redshift calibration, multiplicative shear biases, intrinsic alignments, and baryonic feedback, and extends the CMB-lensing analysis to $L_{\max}=5000$. For fixed $\Sigma m_\nu$, we forecast uncertainties of $\sigma(H_0)\simeq 1.5$ km/s/Mpc with our least informative prior set, $\sigma(H_0)\simeq 1.3$ km/s/Mpc with more restrictive priors, and $\sigma(H_0)\simeq 0.7$ km/s/Mpc with an additional informative prior on the amplitude of primordial fluctuations $A_s$. Allowing $\Sigma m_\nu$ to vary weakens the $H_0$ constraints, while still yielding a forecast 95% CL upper limit of about 0.4 eV on $\Sigma m_\nu$. We also clarify the scope of the $r_d$-agnostic approach through validation tests using mock data from modified recombination and early dark energy models. The method recovers unbiased constraints on $H_0$ for the former but not the latter, highlighting its dependence on assumptions about early-Universe physics.
